Shorts vslong-form

Short-form finds the audience. Long-form keeps it. Why we never plan one without the other, and how a single shoot day can feed both for weeks.

Alex Bull
Co-founder · Creative Director

There's a recurring argument about whether businesses should be making Shorts, Reels and TikToks, or proper long-form video. It's the wrong question. They do completely different jobs, and the brands growing fastest are using them together.

Short-form is discovery

A thirty-second clip can reach thousands of people who have never heard of you. It's the cheapest way to get in front of a new audience, and the platforms are actively pushing it. But it's shallow by design. Someone who watches a Short has given you half a minute. That's a first impression, not a relationship.

Long-form is trust

A twenty-minute YouTube video or a full podcast episode is where people actually get to know you: how you think, how you talk, whether you know your stuff. The audience is smaller, but they stay. They subscribe. They come back. And when they're ready to buy, they already feel like they know who they're buying from.

"Short-form finds the people. Long-form gives them a reason to stay."

One shoot, both formats

This is where making the content and distributing it in the same team pays off. We plan the long-form and the short-form together, before anything's filmed. One podcast or studio day becomes a full episode for YouTube, plus a run of clips cut specifically for Instagram, TikTok and Facebook, each one reframed vertically, captioned and hooked for the platform it's going on.

  • One shoot day produces weeks of content across every channel
  • Clips point people back to the full episode
  • The clips that perform tell you what the next long-form should be about

The mistake to avoid

Don't make long-form and then ask someone else to "chop it up" afterwards. Clips cut from content that wasn't planned for them always feel like offcuts. When the short-form is designed in from the start, the hooks, the framing and the moments are there waiting to be used.

If you're only doing one of the two, you're either reaching people who never stick around, or talking to the same small audience forever.
